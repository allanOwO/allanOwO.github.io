Kyomen

Don't let the weight of your past collapse the world. A fast-paced isometric arena combat game built around dynamic mask-based gameplay, started as a Global Game Jam 2026 entry and now in development by Marmot Studios for Steam.

What I built

  • Designed and built a four-phase boss fight on state machines, with attack selection that filters by range and angle to the player and avoids repeating the last attack, and coroutine-based timing.
  • Built the attack telegraph system: fill indicators built as procedural meshes for each attack shape, with a wind-up on every boss attack so players can read what is coming.
  • Built the transitions between boss phases, including the explosive set piece that marks each escalation.
  • Designed and implemented the enemy wave system to control encounter pacing.
  • Built the first versions of the four enemy types during the jam (melee, ranged, explosive and charger), with separation steering and edge avoidance on a floor that rises and falls.
  • Implemented boss attack patterns and reactive decision-making that respond to the player's actions.
  • Iterated from jam prototype to a playable demo.

My role

I work on the combat programming that drives the game's encounters: escalating pressure, how enemies approach the player, and a boss reactive enough that fights feel less predictable. Since the jam I've spent as much time on readability as on behaviour, making sure every boss attack is telegraphed clearly enough to feel fair. After the jam, the team of five formed Marmot Studios Ltd to take the game to release.

What I learned

Designing gameplay systems under a tight deadline, then hardening them for a real release. Balancing reusable enemy logic against the specific needs of a boss encounter, and learning that an attack is only as good as its telegraph.

How it works

Phase transitions as set pieces

Every time the boss crosses a health threshold it stops what it is doing, travels to the centre of the arena and runs one set-piece attack before the next phase begins. Which attack plays is decided by the phase being left, not by the threshold number. Each one is the outgoing phase's idea pushed further, so the pairing stays right even when the phase order is shuffled.

Invulnerable without feeling broken

The boss cannot be hurt during a transition. Instead of letting hits pass through it, the health bar goes grey and hits still land with all their usual feedback but read 0. The player can see their attacks are registering and simply doing nothing, rather than wondering if the game has bugged out.

Telegraphs that match the hit

Every ground telegraph is built from the same numbers the hit check uses, so the red area is exactly the area that hurts. Shapes are flat convex polygons (circle, cone, lane, trapezoid) drawn as a faint base, a fill that grows out from the attack's origin, and a border drawn inside the true edge so it never overstates the danger.

Patterns that are always escapable

The Bomber Barrage lobs explosive enemies onto a 5 by 5 tile grid using patterns a designer can author in the Inspector. Each pattern is checked before it is used: every targeted tile must have a safe tile directly beside it. A pattern that fails is skipped with a warning instead of producing an unfair hit.
